# Scientific synthesis

## Answer to the study question

The Fakenham record contains recurrent low-flow droughts with substantial differences in duration, deficit volume and antecedent hydroclimatic state. Pronounced episodes occurred in 1976, 1991–92, 1996, 2011 and 2022. The coverage-screened record suggests that days below the 1991–2020 Q90 threshold were more frequent after the mid-1980s than in 1967–1985, but the block-bootstrap interval for the latest-minus-earliest contrast includes zero. The available evidence therefore does not establish a clear long-term change between the first and last study periods.

## Quantitative evidence

- Q90 is 0.274 m³/s using accepted observations in 1991–2020; the good-only estimate is 0.273 m³/s.
- The analysis identifies 123 uncensored Q90 events through 2023. The largest uncensored deficit began on 29 June 1991, lasted 143 days and accumulated 1.197 Mm³, although incomplete annual coverage requires caution.
- Mean annual days below Q90 were 12.8, 31.4 and 30.4 across the three equal periods after excluding years below 95% accepted coverage.
- The latest-minus-earliest contrast was +15.7 days per year, with a three-year block-bootstrap 95% interval from −7.4 to +40.8 days per year.
- Of 123 uncensored events, 39 followed a seasonally low 180-day regional rainfall total. Groundwater context was available near 93 event starts; 21 of those observations were seasonally low.

## Hydrological interpretation

The event comparisons indicate more than one pathway to low river flow. The 1976 episode followed exceptionally dry regional rainfall, whereas the major 1991 and 1996 deficits coincided more clearly with low nearby Chalk groundwater at onset. Some events show neither indicator below its 20th percentile. This heterogeneity is consistent with delayed storage and release in a groundwater-influenced catchment, but it also reflects the limitations of a regional rainfall series and one irregularly sampled borehole.

The study cannot separate climatic forcing from groundwater abstraction, other artificial influences or changes in hydrometric performance. It consequently describes observed drought behaviour at the gauge rather than attributing a climate trend or representing operational drought status for an Anglian Water resource zone.

## Robustness and evidence limits

Threshold, quality-screen and pooling sensitivities have been calculated rather than hidden. Events touching missing or rejected observations are censored, years below 95% coverage are excluded from the period comparison, and sampling uncertainty is represented with a block bootstrap. No missing flow, rainfall or groundwater values are interpolated.

The main spatial limitation is precipitation representation. HadSEEP supplies a long, openly available regional comparator but is not catchment-average Wensum rainfall. A future analysis could extract HadUK-Grid rainfall over the catchment after free CEDA registration. A stronger attribution study would also require abstraction information, additional boreholes and an explicit groundwater or rainfall–runoff model; those extensions are outside this study's present evidence base.
